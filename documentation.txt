Large-scale disaster management requires real-time coordination across thousands of incidents and resources, yet traditional emergency platforms suffer from fragmented communication, static allocation, and poor adaptability under rapidly evolving conditions.

We propose HelpGuru, a cloud-native, event-driven emergency response platform enabling intelligent incident management and dynamic resource optimization. HelpGuru uses a modular microservices architecture where independent services handle incident intake, resource tracking, decision-making, dispatch, authentication, and monitoring, communicating asynchronously through an event-streaming pipeline that ensures high throughput, scalability, and fault tolerance as incident volume grows.

At its core is an explainable multi-factor decision engine that continuously evaluates incident severity, affected population, time sensitivity, travel time, resource availability, environmental conditions, and operational constraints to generate optimal, conflict-free dispatch decisions. Distributed locking and transactional allocation prevent double-booking of resources, while the engine automatically re-optimizes assignments in near real-time as incidents evolve, resources fail, or new emergencies arrive.

Data is managed through a polyglot persistence layer: a relational store for transactional incident and resource records, a geospatial index for location-based matching, and a distributed cache for low-latency reads. Service redundancy, health-checked failover, and event replay keep the platform operational during partial outages.

HelpGuru further ensures security through role-based access control and end-to-end encryption, with comprehensive monitoring and containerized deployment enabling horizontal scalability, rapid failure recovery, and continuous availability under heavy load.

By unifying explainable decision-making with resilient, event-driven architecture, HelpGuru establishes a practical foundation for next-generation, country-wide emergency response management.
